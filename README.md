# Sharjeel Tahir, PhD

**AI Researcher · PhD in Artificial Intelligence**

I work on artificial intelligence, with a particular interest in how intelligent systems understand and respond to human behaviour.

My doctoral research, *Novel Computational Frameworks for Empathy Modelling and Evaluation in Artificial Agents*, investigated how empathy can be computationally modelled, implemented and evaluated in artificial agents. This involved developing and investigating approaches to artificial empathy, multimodal emotion understanding and human–agent interaction.

My work spans academic research and applied AI development, including research at the University of Western Australia, industry projects at Telstra, and robotics experimentation involving ROS-based systems.

I currently teach computer science at the University of Notre Dame Australia and Edith Cowan University.

---

## Research

### Artificial Empathy & Human–AI Interaction

My PhD examined a fundamental question: **What does it mean for an artificial agent to demonstrate empathy, and how can we meaningfully evaluate it?**

Rather than treating empathy as simply detecting emotions or generating sympathetic responses, my research investigated computational frameworks for modelling empathic behaviour and approaches for evaluating artificial agents.

Areas explored include:
- Computational modelling and evaluation of artificial empathy
- Emotion-aware conversational agents
- Multimodal approaches incorporating language and visual information
- Human–agent interaction and empathic communication

### Multimodal AI & Emotion Understanding

My research has included work on combining linguistic and visual information to model empathy in conversational interactions.

This includes E-THER, where I investigated multimodal approaches to empathy modelling, alongside exploratory experiments involving speech representations and audio processing.

### Robotics & Embodied AI

My robotics work has involved the LoCoBot mobile platform, a WidowX 250 robotic arm and ROS-based development.

I'm particularly interested in how intelligent agents can move beyond interpreting human behaviour towards responding appropriately in interactive, embodied environments.
